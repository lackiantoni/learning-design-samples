The Concept

Why Reshaping Your Own Text Is AI's Strongest Move

Generative AI is genuinely good at one thing managers need constantly: taking text you already have and reshaping it. Summarizing, restructuring, pulling out themes, suggesting questions. This is its strongest, safest use, because the model works from your input rather than reaching into its training data to invent facts.

What AI does well here

Takes your messy notes and finds the pattern across them. Returns a structured prep brief with recurring themes and suggested discussion questions.

What you still do

Add the context AI cannot see: the team vacancy, the personal situation, the relationship history. Fix anything vague. Decide what actually matters.

The non-negotiable catch

If your notes describe a concern in vague language, the model hands the vagueness right back. Output quality tracks input quality, every time.

The Move

How to Run the Triage

Step 1: Pull your notes
Gather your rough notes from the last two or three one-on-ones with this direct report. They do not need to be clean. Fragments, bullet points, and half-sentences are fine.
Step 2: Sanitize before pasting
Remove full names and identifying details. Describe the person generically ("a mid-level analyst," "my direct report in operations"). This is the privacy rule you will formalize in the final module; start it now.
Step 3: Prompt for the brief
Ask: "Here are my sanitized notes from recent one-on-ones with a direct report. What patterns appear across the notes, and what are three discussion questions I could raise that help them find the root cause themselves?"
Step 4: Add what the AI cannot know
Read the output. Add the context it does not have: the team situation, the workload reality, the relationship nuance. Drop any question that does not fit the actual person. The AI prepared you. You run the conversation.

The rule to hold onto: AI is working from your input rather than inventing. That is what makes this safe and useful. The moment you ask it to generate facts it does not have, the risk picture changes completely. We cover that in the next lesson.

In Practice

Sam's Ten-Minute Triage

Sam's story

Sam has ten minutes before a one-on-one and pastes in three weeks of notes about a direct report who keeps slipping on documentation. The AI returns a tidy summary and three reasonable questions. It is genuinely helpful. Sam reads it and catches what it missed: this report has been covering a teammate's work since someone left, which is the actual reason the documentation is slipping. Sam adds that context, drops one of the generic questions, and walks in prepared rather than scripted. The AI saved ten minutes. Sam's judgment saved the conversation.

Apply It Today

Run the triage before your next one-on-one

Paste your recent notes for one upcoming one-on-one into an AI tool and ask for a short prep brief with three questions. Then add the one piece of context the AI does not have. Use a sanitized version of the notes, no full names, no sensitive personal details.

Reflect

  • What did the AI get useful, and what did it get vague or generic?
  • What did you have to add that the AI had no way of knowing?
