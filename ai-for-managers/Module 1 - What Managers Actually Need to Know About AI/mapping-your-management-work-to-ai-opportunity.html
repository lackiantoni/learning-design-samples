Task Audit

Mapping Your Work to AI Opportunity

You have limited time and attention. The payoff comes from pointing AI at the right tasks and keeping it away from the wrong ones. A quick audit of your own recurring work tells you where to start and where not to go, so you are not guessing task by task.

The Framework

Three Zones for Every Management Task

Sort your recurring management tasks along one practical axis: how much does this task depend on context, relationship, and judgment that only a human in your seat can supply? That gives three rough zones, and you can place almost any task in one of them.

Augment freely

High-volume, lower-stakes, text-heavy

AI's speed helps and a mistake is cheap and easy to catch. Start here.

  • First drafts of routine status updates
  • Summarizing your own notes
  • Generating options to consider
  • Restructuring a rough plan
Augment with care

Stakes or relationship are real

AI can prepare or draft but a human must own the final output.

  • Feedback drafts
  • Coaching prep and development questions
  • Decision options on operational calls
Keep human

Relationship, trust, emotion, or accountability

AI can help you think beforehand, but the task itself stays yours.

  • Delivering hard news
  • Navigating conflict
  • Repairing trust
  • High-stakes people or legal decisions

In Practice

Dana's Fifteen-Minute Audit

Dana's story

Dana lists a week of recurring tasks and sorts them. Status report drafts and meeting-note summaries go in "augment freely," and Dana starts there, reclaiming a few hours. Drafting a tricky stakeholder message goes in "augment with care," AI drafts, Dana owns the tone. A conversation with a teammate who is frustrated about being passed over goes firmly in "keep human." The audit took fifteen minutes and gave Dana a map: where to push AI hard, where to keep a hand on the wheel, and where to keep AI out of the room.

Apply It Today

Run your task audit

List five recurring tasks from your real week and drop each into one of the three zones. Pick the single "augment freely" task you will try with AI first, and do it before your next one-on-one.

Reflect
