One-on-One Prep

Preparing Richer One-on-Ones With AI Support

The one-on-one is your highest-leverage recurring conversation, and most are under-prepared because prep takes time you do not have. AI gives you a way to review history, spot what is recurring, and arrive with questions that go somewhere, in minutes instead of the half-hour you would never spend.

The Workflow

Four Steps From Notes to Prep Brief

1. Pull your sanitized notes
Gather notes from recent sessions with this direct report. Strip names and identifying details before they go anywhere. You are working from behavioral observations, not personal data.
2. Ask for recurring themes and escalations
Prompt: "What pattern do you see across these notes, and what are three coaching questions I could ask to help them identify the root cause themselves?" This surfaces what you would miss in a five-minute pre-meeting scan.
3. Add context the AI does not have
Add the team vacancy, the workload situation, the relationship history, and anything else the AI had no access to. This is the non-negotiable step that turns a draft into a plan.
4. Adapt the questions to the actual person
Rewrite at least one AI-generated question to fit the real person and their real situation. If a question would feel like a corner rather than an opening, change it. The AI prepped you. You run the conversation.

The rule stated plainly: AI preps the manager. The manager runs the conversation. No AI-generated question should go into a one-on-one unread and unadapted.

Privacy Checkpoint

Same as always, and worth repeating because this is where people slip: sanitize before you paste. No names, no IDs, no private disclosures. Describe the pattern, not the person's identity.

Sam's story

Sam has notes from six monthly one-on-ones with a report who keeps missing documentation deadlines. Sam sanitizes them and asks for the pattern plus three coaching questions. The AI names the pattern (recurring low urgency, no personal deadline-tracking system) and offers good questions. Sam adds the missing context: this report is overloaded because the team is short a person, and reshapes the questions to open a conversation about workload rather than to corner someone about discipline. The AI prepared Sam in five minutes. Sam runs a conversation that actually helps.
