Career Development

Career Conversations in an AI-Transformed Workplace

Your people are anxious about AI and their careers, whether they say so or not. Career conversations are where you either help them see a path or leave them to worry alone. AI can give these conversations real structure: which skills transfer, which are durable, which are at risk.

Skill Adjacency

A Concrete Frame for Career Conversations

Skill adjacency is the degree of overlap between the skills in someone's current role and the skills required for an adjacent role. In a career conversation, you can ask AI to help map which skills transfer directly (durable), which are at risk from automation, and which need building. That turns a vague "where do you want to go?" into something concrete.

Durable skills

Transfer across roles and are hard to automate: judgment, relationships, facilitation, complex communication.

At-risk skills

High-volume, rule-based, or pattern-matching tasks that AI is increasingly handling: data formatting, routine summarizing, template drafting.

Skills to build

The gap between where someone is and the adjacent role they want. Specific, not generic, and identified together in the conversation.

The Prompt

How to Prepare the Skill Map

Prep prompt

"Help me prepare for a career conversation. Here is the role this person is in and the direction they are interested in: [details]. Map which of their current skills transfer directly to adjacent roles, which are most exposed to automation, and what specific skills would be worth building. Use this as a discussion frame, not a verdict."

Dana's story

Dana has a report who is quietly worried that AI will erode the value of their core skill. Dana prepares by asking AI to map skill adjacencies: which skills transfer to adjacent roles, and which are most exposed. The map gives Dana a concrete frame. In the conversation, Dana and the report use it together, not as a verdict but as a starting point, to identify two durable strengths to lean into and one new skill worth building. The report leaves with a direction instead of a worry. The AI structured the thinking. Dana and the report did the deciding, together.

Apply It Today

Sketch a skill map for one report

For one report, sketch (with AI's help) which of their skills are durable and which are more exposed. Bring it to your next career conversation as a discussion starter, not a conclusion.

Reflect

  • Which of your reports is most likely worried about their future and not saying it?
  • What durable strength does one of your people undervalue in themselves?
