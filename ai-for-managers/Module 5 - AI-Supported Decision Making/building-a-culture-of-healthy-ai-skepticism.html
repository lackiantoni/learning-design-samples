Team Culture

Building a Team Culture of Healthy AI Skepticism

Your habits set the team's. If you visibly treat AI output as authority, your team will too, and automation bias spreads from one person to a whole group's decisions. If you visibly verify, question, and name the human input, you build a team that uses AI as a tool rather than an oracle. Culture is downstream of what you model.

Three Moves

Build the Habit Without Building Resistance

Role-model critical evaluation

Out loud, show your work: "The AI suggested this, here is what I verified, here is the context I added." Make the verification visible so the team learns the habit by watching. It takes one sentence per meeting to model this consistently.

Create verification norms

A simple team expectation: no AI recommendation gets acted on without stating what human check was applied. This makes verification a default, not an afterthought, and gives everyone permission to ask "what did we verify?"

Name the drift

The risk is gradual: AI slowly becoming a source of authority rather than a thinking tool. Name that drift when you see it, gently, so the team stays aware that fluency is not the same as truth. "Good starting point: what did we verify?" is the reflex to build.

Dana's story

Dana notices a teammate citing "the AI said so" as the basis for a recommendation in a planning meeting. Rather than scolding, Dana models the better move: "Good starting point: what did you verify, and what context did you add?" Over a few weeks, that question becomes the team's reflex. People start volunteering their verification before being asked. The norm stuck because Dana modeled it consistently, not because Dana wrote a policy.
