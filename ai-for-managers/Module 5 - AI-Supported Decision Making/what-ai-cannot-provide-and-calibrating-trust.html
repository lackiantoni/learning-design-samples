Trust Calibration

What AI Cannot Provide and Calibrating Trust

Every decision has a swing factor: the thing that actually determines the right call. Sometimes AI can help with it. Often it cannot, because the swing factor is human. Knowing which of the four human inputs is decisive, and running a quick trust calibration, is what keeps you from deciding on a confident answer that was missing the one thing that mattered.

The Four Human Inputs

What AI Cannot Supply, No Matter How Fluent It Sounds

Organizational context

What is actually happening here: the politics, the history, the unwritten rules. AI knows only what you typed.

Relationship dynamics

Who trusts whom, who will champion or resist, the human reality the decision lands in.

Ethical accountability

The responsibility for the outcome. This is yours and cannot be delegated to a tool, ever.

Implementation reality

Whether this can actually be executed given your real constraints: capacity, team, budget, timing.

The Calibration Routine

Three Questions Before Acting on Any AI Recommendation

Q1

Is this a well-documented domain?

AI is more reliable on common, well-documented topics. Less reliable on novel, niche, or interpersonal situations specific to your organization.

Q2

Is this a low-stakes output?

The higher the stakes, the more verification it needs before you act on it. Match scrutiny to consequence.

Q3

Am I under time pressure?

Time pressure is exactly when automation bias strikes. Flag it and slow down deliberately. Rushing past this question is the mechanism of the bias itself.

Sam's story

Sam is deciding how to restructure responsibilities on the team and gets a clean, confident AI recommendation. Sam runs the calibration: the domain is this specific team (not well-documented for the AI), the stakes are high (people's roles), and Sam is rushing before a deadline: all three flags up. Then Sam checks the swing factor and realizes it is relationship dynamics. One of the people the AI proposes to shift responsibilities onto is already overloaded and quietly looking to leave, which the AI had no way to know. The recommendation looked complete and was missing the one thing that mattered. Sam supplies it and decides differently.

Apply It Today

Name the swing factor

On your next AI-assisted decision, run the three-question calibration out loud, then name the one human input that is the swing factor. Make sure it is supplied by a human.
