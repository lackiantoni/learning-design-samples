First Drafts

Building Faster, More Complete First Drafts

A blank page is expensive. Reacting to a draft is cheap. AI flips planning from "create from nothing" to "react and improve," which is faster and produces better plans because you spend your energy editing judgment in rather than generating structure from scratch.

Four Draft Types

What AI Supplies and What You Add

Project planAI drafts the structure, milestones, and dependencies. You add the organizational reality: what the team can actually deliver, the political constraints, and the non-negotiable dependencies.
OKR setAI generates candidate objectives and measurable key results from your rough priorities. You judge whether the objective reflects what actually matters for your strategy, context, and team capacity. That assessment is yours every time.
Status reportAI compresses your bullet points into a coherent narrative. You verify the framing before sending and add the political context that shapes how leadership will read it.
Stakeholder communicationAI drafts the same message in multiple tones for different audiences. You select and personalize based on the relationship, the history, and what each stakeholder most needs to hear.

The general rule: AI supplies structure and breadth. You supply organizational context, relationship history, and political reality. The draft is a starting point, never a plan you can ship unread.

Sam's story

Sam needs quarterly OKRs and has always dreaded the blank page. Sam gives AI a paragraph of rough priorities and asks for three candidate objectives, each with measurable key results. The AI returns a solid first draft in seconds. Sam keeps one objective nearly intact, rewrites another because it optimizes for something the team is already good at instead of what the org actually needs, and cuts the third entirely because it ignores a capacity constraint the AI had no way to know about. Thirty minutes total, versus the two hours Sam used to spend just getting started.

Apply It Today

React to a draft instead of starting from scratch

Take one planning artifact you owe (a status report, a plan outline, an OKR set), give AI a rough-input prompt, and edit the draft rather than writing from scratch. Note the time difference.

Reflect

  • What did the AI draft get structurally right that saved you time?
  • What did you have to change because the AI did not know your strategy, capacity, or politics?
