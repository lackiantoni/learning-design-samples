Your Prompt Library

Building a Personal Prompt Library

A prompt that works once is a lucky break. A prompt that works every time is a reusable tool. The managers who get lasting value keep a small library of the few that reliably work for their recurring jobs. This lesson sends you out with three you can use immediately, not a homework folder you will never open.

Your Three Starter Prompts

One Per Course Domain, Ready to Copy

Copy these into a document you actually use and adapt the bracketed parts. The library is three prompts, not thirty, and you actually use all of them. That is the point: small, reused, refined.

Coaching

One-on-one prep

"You are helping me prepare for a one-on-one. Here are my sanitized notes from recent sessions with a direct report: [notes]. Identify the recurring pattern and give me three coaching questions that help them find the root cause themselves. Do not include names."

Planning

Planning risk scan

"Here is a plan I am considering: [plan]. List the ways it could realistically fail, rank them by likelihood, and tell me which single assumption would break first."

Decisions

Decision pre-mortem

"I am about to decide [decision]. Assume it failed badly six months from now. List the most likely reasons it failed, focused on this specific situation, not generic risks."

Diagnose the Prompt

Pair 1

Weak

"Write a development plan for my employee."

Stronger

"Write a 90-day development plan for a mid-level analyst who wants to move into a senior role. Focus on project ownership and stakeholder communication. Structure it as three milestones with success criteria."

Which improvement lever made the biggest difference?

Pair 2

Weak

"Help me prepare for a difficult conversation."

Stronger

"You are a management coach. Help me prepare for a conversation with a report who consistently misses deadlines. Give me three coaching questions that open root-cause inquiry rather than accusation. Keep each question under 15 words."

Which improvement lever made the biggest difference?

Pair 3

Weak

"What are the risks in this plan?"

Stronger

"Here is the plan: [plan text]. List the top five ways this could fail in the next 90 days. For each, name the single assumption that would break first. Rank by likelihood, most likely first."

Which improvement lever made the biggest difference?
