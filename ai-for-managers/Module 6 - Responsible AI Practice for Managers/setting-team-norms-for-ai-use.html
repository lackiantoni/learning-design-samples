Team Norms

Setting Team Norms for AI Use

Everything in this course lands or dies at the level of team norms. A norm your team genuinely shares does more than any policy document, because it lives in the daily work. Establishing it does not require a workshop or a policy rollout. It takes one fifteen-minute conversation, run well, and then your consistent follow-through.

The 15-Minute Conversation

A Real Conversation to Hold With Your Actual Team

This is not a simulation. Schedule it on your real calendar with your real team. The structure below takes fifteen minutes and changes actual behavior.

3 minutes: Opening question

What feels useful, and what feels uncertain?

"When you think about using AI tools for your work, what feels useful, and what feels uncertain or risky to you?" Let people actually answer. Do not fill the silence.

5 minutes: Three ground rules

The non-negotiables

Share three non-negotiables:

1. No employee personal data in public AI tools, ever.
2. No AI-generated content shared externally without a human review pass.
3. No AI recommendation acted on without stating what human check was applied.

5 minutes: Agreement question

One clear "yes" and one clear "human decides"

"What is one situation where we agree AI is clearly helpful, and one where we agree a human should always decide?" Capture both answers on a shared page. Two clear answers beat a long policy nobody remembers.

2 minutes: Closing commitment

Permission to ask first

"If you are ever unsure whether an AI use is appropriate, ask me before, not after." This one sentence does more than the rest of the conversation, because it makes uncertainty safe to surface.

Sam's story

Sam holds the fifteen-minute conversation with the team. The opening question surfaces something Sam did not know: two people were already pasting meeting notes with names into a public tool, not out of carelessness but because nobody had ever said not to. The ground rules fix it on the spot, without blame. The team agrees on one clear "AI is helpful here" (first drafts of routine docs) and one clear "human decides here" (anything about a person's rating or role). It took fifteen minutes and changed the team's actual behavior, which a policy document never had.
